After the Digital Tornado

The Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania · Philadelphia, PA
November 17–18, 2017

About

Networks powered by algorithms are eating everything. Many contemporary technology trends—Internet of Things, Big Data, Platform Economy, Blockchain, and Algorithmic Society—are manifestations of this larger phenomenon. Growing tensions around governance, innovation, surveillance, competition, consumer/worker protection, privacy, and discrimination are best understood within a broader frame. The algorithmic networked world poses deep questions about power, freedom, fairness, and human agency.

Through the conference and associated edited volume, a distinguished interdisciplinary group of scholars considered the challenges and opportunities as the physical and digital worlds merge.

Origin

In 1997, the U.S. Federal Communications Commission published Digital Tornado: The Internet and Telecommunications Policy, one of the first examinations by a government agency of the transformative potential of the internet. The paper was written by Kevin Werbach, then Counsel for New Technology Policy at the FCC.

Twenty years later, little remains untouched by the wave of digital connectivity that paper anticipated. Yet fundamental questions remain unresolved, and new questions have emerged.

The Edited Volume

The conference resulted in the book After the Digital Tornado: Networks, Algorithms, Humanity, edited by Kevin Werbach, published by Cambridge University Press in 2020.

Contributors include leading scholars in law, policy, technology, and business examining how algorithmic networks are reshaping society.

Organizer

Kevin Werbach is Professor of Legal Studies and Business Ethics at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania.
